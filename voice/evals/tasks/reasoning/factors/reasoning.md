For reasoning cases, "reasoning" is the central factor. The answer must work
from looked-up facts (rules, current policy, reported outcomes), enumerate and
compare the realistic alternatives, give concrete pros and cons, and give a
calibrated view of likelihood grounded in sources — not speculation.

A single confident recommendation with no alternatives and no evidence scores
1. An answer that weighs alternatives, cites what it found, and says what its
assumptions are scores 3.
